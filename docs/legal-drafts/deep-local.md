## 2. Privacy policy: new paragraph under "Your leads"

> **Deep research (optional, per campaign).** If our customer turns on Deep research, FirstLine searches the public web for professional information about the contact named in their list: talks, podcasts, interviews, articles and company announcements. We check that a result is about the same person (company, job title, location) before using it; if we're not sure, we use nothing. We never use LinkedIn, and never information about family, health, political views, religion or home address. What we find is stored with the lead and deleted with it, 30 days after the campaign ends. Our customer decides to use Deep research and is responsible for telling the people they email where the information came from.

## 3. DPA: changes

- Section 2 "Data and people", add: "For Deep research: public professional information about the contact (talks, publications, interviews, company announcements) with its source URL."
- Section 3 "Purpose", add: "With Deep research turned on: searching the public web for professional information about the named contact and checking it refers to the same person."
- New clause 4.x: "Deep research excludes special categories of personal data (GDPR Art. 9) and data about criminal convictions (Art. 10), private life, home address and family. The processor discards results it cannot confidently match to the contact."
- Controller obligation (new clause): "The controller has a lawful basis for Deep research on each contact (normally legitimate interest, after a balancing test) and informs the contacts as required by GDPR Art. 14, including the categories of data and their public sources."

## 4. Terms of service: addition to "Your responsibilities"

> If you turn on Deep research, you confirm you have a lawful basis to research the people on your list for business outreach and that you will tell them where the information came from when you contact them (GDPR Art. 14). Don't use Deep research to profile people for any other purpose.
